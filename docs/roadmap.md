Emulating the cat task's draw `0x8c011198` (task 7 at `0x8c1d9d98` in local/hd5/p_02390.ram, `tools/oracle/dc_ram_trace.py`) shows it sets no light either: push, njTranslateV(task+20 = (18.5, 0, 2.5)), two rotations (-0x2000 tilt, -0xc000 facing), then object `0x8c0878c4` through `0x8c0e0938` / `0x8c0fc308`, the mouse's structure. Negative: the mouse's light at 0.55/0.8 of its strength made the cat crop 4.15 (from 2.59). The light is settled: the Ninja light block is `0x8c2d0600` (direction, -diffuse -0.8, ambient - 1 = -0.7), its setters `0x8c0e85d4` / `0x8c0e8614` / `0x8c0e8628` have one reference each, all from `0x8c018d9c`, and the Hard 05 dump holds exactly that light. So the cat is lit by the scene light, and since the port's cat gets worse with it, the port's cat normals or their transform differ from the original. Negatives: every cat node has scale 1 (so the exporter's normalising of normals is not the gap), and face normals rebuilt from the stream's view-space positions do not track the intensities (n.L 0.90 -> 0.775, 0.16 -> 0.60), so the shading comes from smooth vertex normals. Next: pair the stream vertices with the exported cat vertices of the same pose and compare the exported normals, rotated by the cat's chain, with the stream intensities.
